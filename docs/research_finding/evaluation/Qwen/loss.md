# Losses Used in Qwen Models

> **Question:** What training objectives are documented for the Qwen language
> and vision-language backbones, and how should their loss values be read?
>
> **Scope:** Qwen3 and Qwen2.5-VL, with Qwen-VLA action and RL objectives kept
> in [VLA/loss.md](../VLA/loss.md). Research checked on 2026-07-21.

## Short answer

Qwen's public reports describe the sequence of training stages more completely
than they disclose the exact production loss. The safe conclusions are:

- Qwen models are autoregressive generators, but the Qwen3 report does not print
  its full next-token objective, label mask, normalization, or coefficients;
- Qwen3 MoE pretraining adds a global-batch load-balancing loss, without a
  published formula or weight;
- Qwen3 post-training uses supervised cold start, GRPO reasoning RL, general RL,
  and both response and logit distillation;
- Qwen2.5-VL uses multimodal pretraining, SFT and DPO, but its report does not
  publish the corresponding equations or DPO coefficient;
- raw loss values are not comparable across model families, stages or datasets
  unless the target tokens and reduction are identical.

## What a language-model loss means

For an autoregressive model, the usual conceptual objective is negative log
likelihood over target tokens:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}
= -\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}}
\log p_\theta(w_i \mid w_{<i}, x),
$$

where $x$ is the visible context and $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of tokens that
receive labels. Lower loss means the model assigns more probability to the
reference tokens under that exact preprocessing and mask.

This equation is an explanatory definition, **not a claim that the Qwen3 report
publishes this exact reduction**. Whether image tokens, prompt tokens, padding,
tool traces or only assistant tokens are included can materially change the
number. Perplexity, when appropriate, is $\exp(\text{mean token NLL})$; it is
only comparable under the same tokenizer, corpus and masking policy.

## Qwen3

### Base pretraining

The Qwen3 technical report describes three pretraining stages: general
pretraining on more than 30 trillion tokens, reasoning-oriented training on
about 5 trillion tokens, and long-context training on hundreds of billions of
tokens. For mixture-of-experts models it explicitly names a **global-batch
load-balancing loss**, intended to prevent token routing from collapsing onto a
small number of experts and to encourage specialization.

The report does **not** publish:

- the exact causal-language-model loss equation or token mask;
- the load-balancing formula and coefficient;
- a z-loss, router entropy term, or any other router regularizer;
- stage-specific production loss weights.

Those details should remain `Unknown`; importing them from another MoE
implementation would turn a generic possibility into a false Qwen-specific
fact. [Qwen3 report, Sections 2–3][qwen3]

### Cold start and Thinking Mode Fusion

Long-chain-of-thought cold start trains on curated reasoning responses. Later,
Thinking Mode Fusion performs continual supervised fine-tuning on both thinking
and non-thinking examples. These stages tell us which targets are learned, but
the report does not specify the supervised likelihood equation, which tokens are
masked, or how the two modes are weighted. [Qwen3 report, Sections 4.1 and
4.3][qwen3]

### Reasoning RL

Qwen3 applies GRPO to 3,995 query-verifier pairs. A verifier supplies a reward
for generated solutions, and the policy is optimized from multiple rollouts.
The report states that training uses large batches and off-policy sampling, but
does not publish the production GRPO equation, KL/entropy coefficients or exact
reward aggregation.

The reported improvement on AIME is an evaluation result, not a loss value. It
shows that the optimization stage changed behavior; it does not reveal the
scale or stability of the internal RL objective. [Qwen3 report, Section
4.2][qwen3]

### General RL rewards

General RL covers more than 20 task types and uses three reward families:

1. rule-based rewards for outcomes that can be checked deterministically;
2. model-based scoring with a reference answer, using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct;
3. a learned scalar reward model when no reference answer is available.

A reward is a training signal, not a benchmark metric and not necessarily the
same quantity as the policy loss. The report does not disclose the optimizer
objective or relative weighting of these reward families. [Qwen3 report,
Section 4.4][qwen3]

### Strong-to-weak distillation

Qwen3 uses two different forms of distillation for smaller models:

- **off-policy distillation:** students learn from responses generated by a
  larger teacher in thinking and non-thinking modes; the exact response loss is
  not printed;
- **on-policy distillation:** a student generates its own sequence, then its
  token logits are aligned with Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B by minimizing a KL
  divergence.

The report says to minimize KL between teacher and student logits, but does not
identify $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)$ direction, temperature, token reduction or
coefficient. It is therefore safer not to reconstruct an equation. The paper
reports that on-policy distillation gave stronger lightweight-model results
with much less GPU time than repeating the full RL pipeline. [Qwen3 report,
Section 4.5 and Table 21][qwen3]

## Qwen2.5-VL

### Multimodal pretraining

Qwen2.5-VL has three documented phases:

| Phase | Trainable modules | Training scale | Sequence length |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Visual pretraining | ViT only | 1.5T tokens | 8,192 |
| Multimodal pretraining | ViT and LLM | 2.0T tokens | 8,192 |
| Long-context pretraining | ViT and LLM | 0.6T tokens | 32,768 |

The report describes the data and schedule, but it does not print separate
contrastive, alignment, next-token or auxiliary objective equations and their
weights. It is therefore unsafe to assert that a CLIP-style loss or a particular
vision-language alignment term was used merely because those losses are common
elsewhere. [Qwen2.5-VL report, Section 2.2 and Table 2][qwen25-vl]

### SFT and DPO

Post-training first uses roughly two million supervised examples, approximately
half text and half multimodal, serialized in ChatML. It then applies Direct
Preference Optimization to image-text and pure-text preference pairs. The ViT
is frozen during both stages.

The report does not publish the SFT equation, DPO equation, DPO $\beta$,
reference-policy details or component weights. The reward models mentioned in
the data section score and filter candidate QA data before SFT; they are not
evidence of a separate RLHF stage. [Qwen2.5-VL report, Section 2.3][qwen25-vl]

## How to log and compare losses

Store component losses separately rather than only a combined scalar:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| objective and paper equation/reference | Prevents two unlike quantities sharing one name |
| dataset and sample mixture | Easy and hard tokens change NLL scale |
| tokenizer and vocabulary revision | Changes token count and probability space |
| labeled-token mask and denominator | Distinguishes all-token from response-only averaging |
| sequence packing and truncation | Alters which targets enter the average |
| loss coefficient and sampling ratio | Both affect gradient contribution |
| train/validation mode and checkpoint step | Makes curves comparable over time |
| downstream benchmark score | Tests whether lower loss improves useful behavior |

Do not compare a Qwen3 token NLL with a DPO objective, GRPO surrogate, reward,
or Qwen-VLA action MSE. Even two token losses can differ because of tokenizer,
label masking and data composition. A loss curve diagnoses optimization under a
fixed contract; [metrics.md](metrics.md) defines the reported score functions,
while [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md) records evaluation protocols and results.
