The thesis

One format, many capture rigs

Five source datasets record humans doing manual work from a head-mounted camera. Each ships a different pose convention, hand representation, frame rate and file layout. The corpus normalises all of them into one per-frame Parquet record so a single dataloader can feed a VLA model from every source at once.

What every frame carries

3D head/camera pose · 3D pose of both hands (wrist + 21 keypoints) · per-hand validity · source-native finger articulation.

What every clip carries

Intrinsics, gravity vector, licence, split + leakage group, stream registry, and the provenance of every transform applied to it.

What rides alongside

Timestamped language: per-hand narration, sub-task captions, and synthesised locomotion commands — 3,977,393 segments.

Three consumers, one store

  • VLA manipulation — action chunks of head motion + both hands at 10 Hz.
  • VLN navigation — head-only locomotion commands from a closed vocabulary.
  • VLM embodied language — narration targets conditioned on their generator.
The governing rule. Layer-1 stores raw metric world-frame state once and never changes it. Every model-specific choice — control rate, chunk horizon, anchor frame, rotation representation, normalisation — is a dataloader-time transform. Nothing model-shaped is baked into a release.

The camera frame

+X → right+Y → DOWN +Z → forward

+Y is down, not up. Measured mean hand position in camera frame is positive in Y for all three sources — hands sit below the optical axis. Assuming Y-up flips every hand label.

u = fx·X/Z + cx
v = fy·Y/Z + cy      (valid for Z > 0)
intrins = [fx, fy, cx, cy]   pixels, per clip

The world frame

World is defined per clip as the camera pose at its first frame, so cam_R[0] = I and cam_t[0] = 0 by construction.

X_cam   = cam_R @ X_world + cam_t     w2c
p_head  = −cam_Rᵀ @ cam_t             head in world
R_c2w   = cam_Rᵀ
gaze    = R_c2w[:, 2]                 optical axis in world
img_up  = −R_c2w[:, 1]
World coordinates are a per-clip gauge. They are never comparable between clips. Anything cross-clip must go through camera-relative or anchor-relative quantities. gravity_up differs for every one of the 380,401 clips — hard-coding [0,−1,0] is wrong for all of them.

egoverse · internal Aria-style · 49,650 clips · 987.0 h

egoverse — native mocap

Internal recordings in zarr: head pose from Aria-style SLAM in a gravity-aligned +Z-up world, hands as 21 mocap keypoints. The hard part is that the pose and the camera are not the same sensor.

The CPF problem

Head pose is reported in the Central Pupil Frame; the video comes from the RGB camera. They differ by a fixed rotation nobody shipped, so hand keypoints projected with the raw pose land off the hands.

Fix: a one-time extrinsic fit (tools/fit_egoverse_extrinsic.py) in two stages — dump candidate frames from zarr, then fit the CPF→RGB rotation by maximising how well projected wrists land inside YOLO-detected hand boxes. Result is a single 3×3 stored in adapters/egoverse_extrinsic.json.

Rotation only. The CPF→RGB translation (lever arm) is never applied — the fit does not model it.

Two storage families, one adapter

A discriminator decides at runtime whether keypoints are already in the world frame:

d_head = ‖wrist_raw − head_raw‖
world_hands = 0.15 < median(d_head) < 1.2

Branch A (world-native): exact, invert the raw pose, no extrinsic.
Branch B (head-fixed): apply M = E @ G_axes, where E is the fitted extrinsic and G_axes is a signed permutation chosen from 24 candidates by a projection score.

Axis auto-detection — 24 candidates

score(M) = mean(front) + mean(inside) + 0.25·central
front  : 0.1 < z < 1.2 m in camera frame
inside : projected uv lands within the image
central: 1 − mean normalised distance from centre
reject if mean(front) < 0.5 or mean(inside) < 0.5

argmax over all 24 proper signed permutation matrices (det = +1). Raises if none survive.

Two incompatible cameras, not one. Measured across 120 clips:
640×360 (39,436 clips) — fx≈fy≈251, isotropic, HFOV ≈ 103.9°
640×480 (10,214 clips) — fx≈174, fy≈209, fy/fx = 1.20
A single "egoverse intrinsics" figure is wrong for one of the families. The 1.20 ratio looks like an unrecorded 480/400 vertical stretch.
79 % of egoverse is labelled task_name = "debug" — 39,436 clips, 782.5 h, correlating perfectly with the 640×360 family. Those clips still carry real narration. Do not build a task-conditioned view on that column.

The 61°-down camera

The record camera points steeply downward — good for hands, bad for heading. Yaw jitter is amplified ×2.1 relative to a level camera, which is why xp10m carries a separate head_body_pose stream at 20 Hz and the action labels override their heading from it rather than from the camera.

Only stream in the entire corpus: 12,696 sidecars, and the only release with a populated streams/ directory.

Left-hand de-mirroring

HaMeR predicts left hands by mirroring the image and running the MANO-right model, so the exported left orientation is claimed to live in an x-mirrored frame. The adapter conjugates it back:

F = diag(−1, +1, +1)         F = Fᵀ = F⁻¹, det F = −1

R_hand_cam = R                 right hand
R_hand_cam = F @ R @ F         left hand

det(F R F) = det(F)²·det(R) = +1   still proper
Measured residual says this fires where it should not. Checking the shipped left global_orient against the shipped left joints gives 0.49 cm on the right hand but 3.15 cm on the left — the source's left orientation already matched its joints, so the conjugation introduces the error it was meant to remove. It flows into rot_cam → action dims 12:18 for every xp10m left hand. kp21 is unaffected (it comes from the joints, not the orientation). Fix if confirmed: R_true = F @ (cam_R @ wrist_rot_l) @ F.
Validity is genuinely low here. Frame-weighted valid rate is 0.658 (L) / 0.670 (R) versus ~1.00 for egodex. Occlusion and dropout are real; mask, never assume.

Resident batching

Stage T · 0 — media conditioning

Transcode and frames

Chunking arithmetic

DUR      = floor(ffprobe duration)        integer seconds
NCHUNKS  = max(1, ceil(DUR / CHUNK))      CHUNK = 300 s
START(K) = K · CHUNK                      seconds
start_frame(K) = START(K) · FPS           on the ORIGINAL fps timeline
SEQ(K)   = "<stem>_c%03d" % K

start_frame is what lets chunks be packaged later as concatenable shots of one orig_id. The floor on duration can drop under a second of tail.

GPU transcode path

NVDEC decode + h264_nvenc encode, with per-codec cuvid decoders for hevc/h264/av1. ViPE never touches the encode/decode silicon, so that capacity is free — the header claims 485× less CPU than libx264 at marginally better PSNR/SSIM.

Dual rendition, one frame set

A downscaled ViPE-only rendition (--vipe-height 540) is emitted from a single decode→fps→split filter graph so both renditions hold exactly the same frames. Frame-count parity is then asserted via ffprobe nb_frames and the run exits FATAL on mismatch.

Why parity is load-bearing. Poses are computed on the 540p rendition but hands on full-res frames. If the two frame sets ever diverge by one, every hand is fitted against the wrong camera pose — silently, with plausible-looking output.

Stage 0

Plain ffmpeg CPU decode to images/<seq>/*.jpg. The extraction rate here must match data.frame_opts.fps in Stage C — wr_adapter._read_clip_fps treats the declared value as authoritative and will not detect the mismatch.

Stage A — ViPE

Camera track

NVIDIA's Video Pose Engine, vendored with 22 local patch sites, produces the per-frame camera-to-world pose and intrinsics that everything else is anchored to.

Outputs

pose/<seq>.npz
OpenCV cam2world 4×4 per frame
intrinsics/<seq>.npz
[fx, fy, cx, cy] per frame, in ViPE's own input pixel units
rgb/, mask/

A clip only counts as done when both npz files exist — vidproc.preprocess_cameras needs the pair.

Local patches that matter

  • VIPE_BACKEND_WINDOW — fixed-lag backend, turns O(t2.7) bundle adjustment into roughly linear. Validated drift < 0.3 % of path.
  • VIPE_BACKEND_DEDUP, VIPE_BACKEND_EVERY — A/B-gated edge-selection savings.
  • Disk-cache spill so long sequences fit in VRAM (SLAM_BUFFER, 3.31 MB per slot).

Never set VIPE_DISK_CACHE_FP16=1 — it changes output. One doc sample env sets it; the README and the full-video handoff both forbid it. The README is authoritative.

Intrinsics rescale — the silent tilt

Downstream stages may decode at a different resolution than ViPE consumed, so every consumer rescales:

W_vipe   = 2 · cx_vipe        assumes centred principal point
s        = W_frame / W_vipe
fx_used  = s · fx_vipe

Dyn-HaMR implements the same rule independently and additionally asserts the aspect ratios agree to 1e-2 before scaling all four entries. Without this the unprojection is off by the resolution ratio and gravity silently tilts.

Validating a pose track

ops/cmp_pose.py compares two trajectories that differ by a Sim(3): Umeyama-align the translations, then report ATE (median/p95/max, absolute and as % of path) plus gauge-free frame-to-frame rotation. This is the acceptance test used as the noise floor when evaluating a ViPE patch.

Stage G — the most mathematical stage

Gravity estimation

One world-UP unit vector per clip. Everything that decomposes motion into forward / right / up depends on it, so it gets three independent estimators and an arbitration gate. Primary source is depth-plane geometry, not IMU.

1 · Sample and unproject

# 40 frames, uniformly spaced in row index
idxs = linspace(0, N−1, 40).astype(int)

# UniDepth-L, resident, conditioned fx = fy = f
D = model.estimate(rgb).metric_depth   # (H,W) metres

cx, cy = W/2, H/2
u, v   = grid(stride_px = 8)
z      = D[v, u]
mask   = isfinite(z) ∧ (0.1 < z < 8.0)

X = [ (u−cx)/fx_used · z,
      (v−cy)/fx_used · z,
      z ]                    # camera coords, metres

fy never appears — the v term divides by fx_used. Correct given UniDepth was conditioned isotropically, but it means ViPE's reported fy is discarded here.

2 · Peel planes by RANSAC

for p in 1..4:
    if |X| < 400: break
    n̂, inl, Xc = ransac(X, thr=0.03 m, iters=250)
    if |inl| < 0.10·|X|: break
    normals += R_c2w(i) @ sign_up(n̂, Xc)
    weights += |inl|
    X = X[¬inl]              # peel and continue

Refit is total least squares: the plane normal is the right singular vector of the smallest singular value of the centred inlier set.

3 · Sign each normal toward the camera

sign_up(n, centroid):
    return −n  if (n · centroid) > 0
           n   otherwise
# post-condition: n_up · centroid ≤ 0

The camera centre is the origin, so UP points from the support surface toward the camera: up ∝ (origin − centroid) = −centroid. This is why the estimator survives steeply down-looking rigs where the naive "camera −Y is up" heuristic fails.

4 · Pool by spherical RANSAC

ct   = cos(12°) ≈ 0.9781
D    = N̂ N̂ᵀ                     SIGNED cosine
best = argmax_i Σ_j w_j·1[D_ij > ct]
inl  = {k : D[best,k] > ct}
axis = unit(Σ_inl w_k n̂_k / Σ_inl w_k)
inl  = {k : n̂_k · axis > ct}     one refit pass
axis = unit(weighted mean again)
spread_deg = P90(angle(n̂_k, axis))
frac = Σ_inl w / Σ_all w

Every horizontal surface — floor, worktop, ironing board — votes the same up direction, so its cluster wins; walls and objects scatter. The cosine is signed, so an antipodal normal is an outlier rather than being folded in — correct only because step 3 pre-signed everything.

Arbitration. Depth-plane, EPIC accelerometer IMU (resampled onto the video clock, 0.5 s boxcar low-pass, brute-force axis map) and trajectory PCA each produce a candidate. select_gravity takes the lowest-spread estimate that passes the 12° gate; if none does, the clip is flagged and its head-derived targets are excluded downstream. Needs ≥6 pooled planes or there is no depth candidate at all.

Pruning policy

With PRUNE=1 derived artefacts are deleted as soon as they are consumed — masks after A, rgb after G, images and optimiser state after C is DONE. Residual drops from ~0.82 GB to ~40 MB per clip, which is what makes a thousand-clip corpus fit on the box at all.

Stage G has no failure file and no summary line, and the driver always exits 0. A gravity daemon that silently produced nothing looks identical to one that succeeded — check for the presence of gravity/<seq>.json, not the exit code.

Reconstruction — branch on the tag

There is no single "reconstruction recipe". The MANO-family version is printed in the README and the math report; it is wrong for egodex, whose fingers are wrist-frame SE(3), not camera-axis xyz.

kp21 formats — egoverse, xp10m

wrist_cam    = cam_R @ trans + cam_t
kp_cam[i]    = wrist_cam + fingers[3i : 3i+3]
kp_world[i]  = trans + cam_Rᵀ @ fingers[3i : 3i+3]

Fingers are already in camera axes, so the camera-frame reconstruction is a plain addition. Going to world needs only the rotation, never the translation — an offset is a direction-like quantity here, anchored at the wrist.

ARKit format — egodex

F        = fingers.reshape(T, 24, 3, 4)
p_j      = F[:, j, :3, 3]          wrist-FRAME position, m

p_world  = trans + wrist_rot @ p_j
p_cam    = cam_R @ p_world + cam_t

# canonical kp21 view:
kp21[j]  = (cam_R @ wrist_rot) @ p_j

The per-joint 3×3 rotation block — 9 of the 12 floats — is discarded by to_kp21.

Why chunk-anchored, not step-to-step

Per-step head motion at 10 Hz is ≲1 cm against mm-scale SLAM jitter — SNR ≈ 1, and errors compound over a rollout. Integrating to the anchor over 1.6 s averages the jitter away. This is the EgoScale ΔW = W₀⁻¹Wₜ convention.

Why 6d rotation

Continuous, no gimbal or double-cover discontinuity to fight during flow matching. Two columns of the rotation matrix; orthonormalise at inference.

Why kp21, not MANO

Two of three sources have no MANO at all, and fitting is far more expensive than a forward pass. MANO stays available natively for future mesh supervision.

d_pos and d_rot live in different frames inside the same 9-dim head block. d_pos is in the gravity-corrected FRU heading frame; d_rot is in the anchor camera frame (OpenCV, +Y down). They share no basis. Confirmed by the xp10m heading override: swapping heading sources moves d_pos by up to 5.2 mm and leaves d_rot bit-identical.

Head target math

1 · Build the anchor heading basis

û   = up / (‖up‖ + 1e-12)        clip gravity_up
g   = H[:, 2]                     forward axis in world
h   = g − (g·û) û                 horizontal projection
n   = ‖h‖

if n < 0.15:                      gaze within 8.6° of vertical
    h = f − (f·û) û               f = fallback_fwd
    n = ‖h‖                       NO second check

fwd   = h / (n + 1e-12)
right = unit(fwd × û)

B0 = [ fwd  ]                     ROWS, not columns
     [ right]
     [  û   ]
B0 is left-handed — det(B0) = −1. It is orthogonal, so norms and dot products are preserved, but you must not treat it as a rotation, compose it with d_rot, or expect right-handed cross-product signs in that frame. The relation is right × fwd = up, not fwd × right = up.

2 · The targets

t_k      = t_0 + stride·(k+1),  k = 0…15
p(t)     = −cam_R(t)ᵀ @ cam_t(t)

d_pos[k] = B0 @ (p(t_k) − p(t_0))       metres, FRU
d_rot[k] = R_c2w(t_0)ᵀ @ R_c2w(t_k)     anchor camera

Gauge-invariant: apply any rigid transform to all poses (and rotate up with it) and both outputs are unchanged. Golden-tested.

3 · xp10m heading override

The record camera points 61° down, amplifying yaw jitter ×2.1, so the anchor heading comes from the body-pose stream instead:

R_w2b(t0)   = quat_to_R(value[t0, 0:4])
R_c2w_src   = (R_b_c0 @ R_w2b(t0))ᵀ
R_clip←src  = cam_R(t0)ᵀ @ R_w2c_src(t0)
H_body      = R_clip←src @ R_w2b(t0)ᵀ

H_body is fed to the basis builder in place of the camera matrix. Positions still come from the record camera — head is the camera.

Effect: rotates the FRU decomposition of d_pos only. ‖d_pos‖ is unchanged (B0 orthogonal) and d_rot is untouched. A consumer who "fixes" tilt by also rotating d_rot double-applies.

Head twist and joystick commands

The navigation channel: reduce head motion to six interpretable rates, then to a closed vocabulary of locomotion commands that tile every clip.

Six twist channels — mixed units

Δp(t) = p(t) − p(t−1)

ch0 FWD    [m/s]   = (Δp · fwd(t)) · fps
ch1 STRAFE [m/s]   = (Δp · right(t)) · fps      + = RIGHT
ch2 UP     [m/s]   = (Δp · û) · fps
ch3 YAW  [deg/s]   = −deg(atan2((fwd(t−1) × fwd(t))·û,
                            fwd(t−1)·fwd(t))) · fps
ch4 PITCH[deg/s]   = (elev(t) − elev(t−1)) · fps
                     elev = deg(arcsin(clip(opt·û, −1, 1)))
ch5 ROLL [deg/s]   = (φ(t) − φ(t−1)) · fps
That leading minus on YAW is deliberate and load-bearing. atan2(cross·up, dot) is naturally positive for a CCW/left turn in this right-handed world; the negation makes +YAW = turn RIGHT. It is the 2026-07-06 sign fix, regression-tested, and all three releases were generated with it. Re-deriving yaw without it inverts every rotate left/rotate right label in the corpus.
Row 0 is structurally zero on all six channels — a padding convention, not a measurement. Exclude it from every statistic. And ROLL is never unwrapped: an atan2 output in (−180°, 180°] is differenced, so a roll crossing the boundary produces a ±360·fps spike. Latent — joystick integrates only channels 0-4.

Windowed net displacement

w      = max(1, round(1.2 s · fps))
net(t) = cum[clip(t+w/2)] − cum[clip(t−w/2)]

cum_fwd/str/vert = cumsum(tw[:,0..2] / fps · scale)   m
cum_yaw/pitch    = cumsum(tw[:,3..4] / fps)           deg

Absolute gates → closed vocabulary

ChannelGateLabels
forward±0.15 mFORWARD / BACKWARD
strafe±0.20 mLEFT / RIGHT
vertical±0.15 mSTAND / CROUCH
turn±13.0°ROT_LEFT / ROT_RIGHT
look±12.0°LOOK_UP / LOOK_DOWN

Window 1.2 s, minimum dwell 0.9 s, then run-length encoded into spans that partition the clip exactly. Strafe is suppressed while turning.

Two threshold tables exist; only one is the spec. The production gates are the absolute ones above. qa/validate_layer1.py carries a different Schmitt-trigger table (0.20/0.10 m/s, 12/6 deg/s, dwell 0.25 s) applied to instantaneous rates — it exists only to prove derivability. Never quote it as the corpus joystick spec. The joystick.py module docstring is also stale: it advertises adaptive gates, and there is no adaptive code path.
Boundary bias. The window truncates at clip edges, so net motion is systematically under-estimated in the first and last ~0.6 s — those spans skew toward STOP/STRAIGHT/LEVEL.

Action normalisation

Pooled per-step per-dim q01/q99 → [−1, 1], one stat set for all three sources. Computed over the training mixture at dataloader time; releases store raw metric values and are never touched.

Derivation

center = (q99 + q01) / 2
half   = (q99 − q01) / 2

# translations: PER-DIM scale
PER_DIM = [(0,3), (9,12), (81,84)]
scale[:, a:b] = half[:, a:b]

# rot6d + kp21: ONE scalar per block per step
BLOCKS = [(3,9),(12,18),(18,81),(84,90),(90,153)]
scale[:, a:b] = half[:, a:b].max(axis=1)

scale = where(scale < 1e-6, 1.0, scale)
dead  = (q99 − q01) < 1e-6
z   = clip((act − center) / scale, −1, 1)
msk = where(dead, 0, msk)
out = where(msk > 0, z, 0)     SELECT, not multiply

Select, never multiply: invalid steps carry filler up to ~400 m and can be non-finite, and NaN × 0 = NaN.

Why granularity differs

The flow loss is unweighted MSE in normalised space, so per-dim scale is a per-dim loss weight of 1/scale². Per-dim on rot6d would give the near-constant diagonal terms (≈1 − θ²/2, noise-dominated) 27× the weight of the informative off-diagonals; per-dim on kp21 would give proximal joints 26× the penalty of fingertips — the inverse of what manipulation needs, and it would whiten rotations and normalise hand size away.

Why per-step, not pooled over the horizon

Head blocks grow ~12× and ~8.8× across the 16 steps while the six per-step hand blocks are flat to within 3 %:

Consumer traps

Frames & media

  • decode index == frame, verbatim, all three sources. The shipped egodex DATASET_CARD's remap formula is the retired v05 recipe and double-shifts by −100k…−660k frames.
  • Three codecs, one loader: AV1 / HEVC / H.264. cv2 without dav1d silently fails or returns garbage on AV1 — use ffmpeg or PyAV.
  • One mp4 is shared by ~66 egodex clips. Dedup by path before probing.
  • xp10m's container claims 25 fps; the sensor is 20 Hz. Take fps from clip meta — ffprobing gives a 25 % timing error on 12,696 clips and silently changes both the action stride and the velocity gate.
  • timestamp is absolute video time, not clip time.

Validity

  • valid == 0 never means NaN. Always mask, never NaN-test.
  • Invalid rows carry filler up to 402 m.
  • has_left == True says nothing about usable frames — 716 egoverse and 169 xp10m clips have zero valid left frames anyway.
  • valid_* are per-frame columns, not manifest columns, and they are scalars, not length-1 lists.

Frames of reference

  • Camera +Y is down.
  • Mixed frames inside one row: trans/wrist_rot are world, fingers are wrist-frame (egodex) or camera-axis (the others), cam_R/cam_t are world→camera while wrist_rot is hand→world.
  • World coordinates are a per-clip gauge — never comparable across clips.
  • d_pos and d_rot are in different frames; B0 is left-handed.
  • +YAW = turn RIGHT by deliberate negation.

Language

  • n_segments > 0 ≠ "has language" — joystick tiles every clip.
  • Narrative spans can overlap; joystick spans partition exactly.
  • gen_model encodes neither prompt version nor sampling rate. The vendored prompt file is byte-identical to the old whole-second v1 prompt while the launch script names the decimal v2 — and the parser's regex silently drops every MM:SS.d line. Which prompt produced qwen36_narrative_v1 is unrecoverable from the artefacts. Say "unknown"; do not assert.
Licence. xp10m is NON-COMMERCIAL and is 37.0 % of the training index / 36.3 % of corpus hours, with terms that explicitly extend to trained weights. Any commercial run must drop it — and dropping it removes 37 % of windows and silently mis-scales the dominant forward axis by 2.12× unless the normalisation stats are regenerated. The loader hard-asserts the release set for exactly this reason.
